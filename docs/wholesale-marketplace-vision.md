# Call Summary: Devin x Geoff — Wholesale Marketplace Vision
**Date:** Feb 13, 2026

## The Big Idea
The WAXED Enterprise wholesale marketplace is basically a consumer shopping experience, but exclusively for B2B buyers (stores, labels, distributors). Think Discogs meets wholesale — clean, search-driven, not a cluttered feed. The person behind the counter is a collector too, so it should feel like shopping, not enterprise procurement.

## Who's Selling
- **Record Labels** (e.g. Sonic Groove) — new release pre-orders and shelf stock at wholesale pricing (~$5-6/record). Minimum order could be 25 units of a single release.
- **Distributors** — pre-orders and existing inventory at wholesale
- **Record Stores** — not just dead stock. Any inventory they choose to list. Could be surplus, could be stuff they're willing to move. It's up to each store how much of their catalog they expose to the network.

## Who's Buying
- Other record stores (primary audience)
- Potentially large collectors — but this gets complicated (see B2B gate below)
- Anyone willing to transact in bulk minimums

## UX Philosophy
- **Search-first, not browse-first.** The store owner knows what they want. Type in "Sonic Groove" or "freestyle" and get clean, relevant results. Don't bombard with suggestions.
- **Filters:** Genre, condition/star rating, quantity, source type (label/distributor/store)
- **Intelligence layer:** Use the store's own analytics (what they sell, what customers search for) to surface relevant wholesale options algorithmically. But let them search further if they want more.
- **Checkout like a normal shop.** Card on file, add to cart, done. No proposal/negotiation flows.

## Minimum Order Thresholds
- **Labels/new releases:** 25 units makes sense (you're not selling 75 of one record)
- **Old inventory/dead stock from stores:** Higher minimums (50-75) because individual records are cheap ($1-3). A store needs volume to make the fee worthwhile — 75 records at $1 is only $75.
- The minimum is also the B2B gate — it's what separates wholesale from retail and protects WAXED's fee economics.

## Request System (Demand Side)
- Stores could post public requests: "I need freestyle, minimum 50 records, 3+ stars"
- Network matches and notifies when a seller has what you need
- Similar to the inbound pack requests we already have, but bidirectional
- Concern: could look bad if requests go unfilled on a quiet network. **Seller-side only might be best for launch.**
- The existing want list / notification system on the consumer side could serve this role for enterprise too

## Searchable Catalog (Discogs-Like)
- Everything should be searchable even if it's not for sale
- Once launched, the full Discogs catalog should be searchable on WAXED
- Requests sit against catalog entries waiting for matches
- This is foundational infrastructure, not just a marketplace feature

## B2B Access Control
- **Problem:** Collectors seeing wholesale/distributor pricing feels invasive and "dirty." If a customer knows a store pays $5 for a record they sell at $18, it undermines trust.
- **Proposed solution:** Require EIN/LLC to access wholesale marketplace. Getting an LLC costs money and effort — enough friction to gate out hobbyist collectors.
- **Consumer-side alternative:** Big collectors get served through WAXED Packs on the consumer app (10/20/30 record bundles with flat-rate shipping). That's their bulk buying channel.

## Store vs. Collector Identity
- Open question: What constitutes a "record store"? Online-only stores, Instagram sellers, serious collectors who flip — the line is blurry.
- EIN requirement is a reasonable minimum barrier for now.

## Devin's Closing Thought
"I have a better idea now of what this needs to be" — he wants to mock it up visually to see if it works. Needs to sit with the B2B access question.
